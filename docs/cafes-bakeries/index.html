What gets missed in small cafés

Display case drift

Glass cases opened all day can creep above 41°F by mid-afternoon. A single morning reading won't catch it.

Cooling cream fillings

Pastry cream and custard are TCS foods. Cooling them on the counter "until later" is a classic inspection finding.

Cleaning rota gaps

With part-time staff, weekly and monthly jobs (ice machine, behind the fridges) are easy to lose track of.

Café & bakery FAQ

Do coffee shops need temperature logs?

If you store milk, sandwiches, salads or other TCS food, it must be held at 41°F or below, and most inspectors want to see you're checking. Whether a written log is required depends on your local health department, but it's the simplest way to show compliance.

Are baked goods TCS food?

Many aren't. Bread, most cookies and fruit pies are usually not TCS. Cream-, custard- and cheese-filled items, cheesecake and meringue-topped pies generally are, so they need cold holding and careful cooling.

Does a small bakery need a HACCP plan?

Under the FDA Food Code, a HACCP plan is required only for certain specialized processes (such as reduced-oxygen packaging or acidifying food for shelf stability) or when you request a variance. Many states also encourage a simpler written food safety plan. Ask your health department.
