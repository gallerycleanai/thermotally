HACCP temperature log: what it is and what inspectors look for

Updated 2026-10-25 · 8 min read · Based on the FDA Food Code

"HACCP temperature log" gets used for any kitchen temperature record. Strictly, it's the monitoring record for a critical control point in a HACCP plan. Knowing the difference tells you how much paperwork you really need.

HACCP in one paragraph

HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) is a system: identify the hazards in your process, choose the points where you can control them (critical control points, or CCPs), set a measurable critical limit for each, monitor it, take corrective action when it's missed, verify the system works, and keep records. The temperature log is where monitoring and corrective actions are written down.

Does a small restaurant need a HACCP plan?

Under the FDA Food Code, a formal HACCP plan is required only in specific cases (§8-201.13): when you apply for a variance, or for certain specialized processes such as reduced-oxygen packaging (sous vide, cook-chill, vacuum packing), smoking or curing for preservation, acidifying sushi rice, or operating a live shellfish tank. Most single-location restaurants, food trucks and cafés don't need a formal HACCP plan.

They still need to meet the same temperature rules, and inspectors still expect monitoring records. Many states and counties also push "active managerial control": written procedures and logs that show you're in control. The 2026 FDA Food Code goes further with a written Food Safety Management System requirement (more in our paper vs digital guide).

What inspectors look for in your logs

  1. Completeness. Every scheduled check has a reading, time and initials.
  2. Plausibility. Numbers vary like real readings do. Twenty identical entries in the same handwriting are a red flag.
  3. Corrective actions. Every out-of-range reading has an action and a re-check.
  4. Verification. A manager reviewed and signed the logs (daily or weekly), and thermometers were calibrated.
  5. Agreement with reality. The log matches what the inspector measures today.

For a HACCP plan, the Code also lists what the plan and its records must contain (§8-201.14), including the monitoring records themselves, corrective actions taken, and verification records.

A simple monitoring routine

  • Opening: all fridges and freezers, thermometer check in ice water (weekly).
  • Deliveries: receiving log.
  • Service: hot and cold holding every 2 hours.
  • After cooking batches for later: start a cooling log.
  • Closing: fridges again, cleaning checklist, manager signs the day.

FAQ

Is "HACCP temperature log" a specific official form?

No. There's no single federal form. Some health departments publish their own forms, and if yours does, use it. Otherwise any log that captures date, time, reading, initials, corrective action and verification works.

How long must HACCP records be kept?

It depends on your jurisdiction and your approved plan. Many plans keep records for at least 6 months to a year, but your health department's requirement is the one that counts.

What's the difference between HACCP and a food safety plan?

A HACCP plan is the formal 7-principle system. "Food safety plan" is often a simpler written set of procedures (SOPs) and logs that many health departments encourage for every operation.
