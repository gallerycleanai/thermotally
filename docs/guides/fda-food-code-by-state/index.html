Which FDA Food Code version does my state use?

Updated 2026-10-25 · 6 min read · Based on the FDA Food Code

Short answer: there's no single national restaurant code. The FDA publishes the Food Code as a model, and each state (and sometimes a county or city) adopts a version of it, often with changes. According to FDA's adoption report covering 2024, 7 states had adopted the newest 2022 Food Code by the end of 2024, 24 states were on the 2022 or 2017 version, and 2 state agencies (Maryland's and New York's health departments) had adopted no version. Your local health department is the final word on which rules apply to you.

Why the version matters (and why it usually doesn't)

The core temperature rules have been the same for many editions: cold holding at 41°F or below, hot holding at 135°F or above, cooling from 135°F to 70°F in 2 hours and to 41°F within 6 hours total, and reheating to 165°F. Differences between versions tend to be in details, such as:

  • Whether a certified food protection manager is required (§2-102.12 in the 2022 Code), and how employees must be trained.
  • Allergen rules: the 2022 Code added sesame as the ninth major allergen and requires written notification to consumers of major allergens in unpackaged food.
  • Date-marking exemptions, time as a public health control details, and specialized processes like sous vide.
  • Older codes (2001 or 2005) have some different terms and fewer of the later changes. Some states also modified their adopted version; FDA's report found 38 of 62 adopting state agencies had at least one area less stringent than the version they adopted.

FDA released a new 2026 Food Code in September 2026; it will take years for states to adopt it, as the table shows. Our guides cite the FDA Food Code 2022. If your state uses an older version, the numbers on this site for temperatures and cooling still apply in almost all cases, but section numbers and details may differ.

Local rules can be stricter

In many states, counties and cities enforce the state code but can add their own rules, and some large cities have their own food codes. FDA's Food Code preface notes there are more than 3,000 local health departments in the US. Rules for food trucks, cottage food, temporary events and permits are especially likely to be local. Check:

  1. Your county or city health department's website for the retail food code it enforces.
  2. Your last inspection report, which usually cites the code sections used.
  3. Your state health or agriculture department's retail food page.

Keeping records that work under any version

Whatever version applies, inspectors check the same basics: cold and hot holding temperatures, cooling, date marking, sanitizer strength and thermometers. A simple set of logs covers all of them; see the restaurant health inspection checklist and our free printable logs.

FAQ

Is the FDA Food Code a law?

Not by itself. It's a model code that FDA recommends. It becomes law when a state, county or city adopts it, often with changes.

What is the latest FDA Food Code?

FDA released the 2026 Food Code in September 2026. States take years to adopt a new edition, so for now most businesses are inspected under the 2022, 2017 or an older version. The core temperature limits cited on this site are the same in the 2026 edition.

Which states use the 2022 Food Code?

Per FDA's 2024 report: Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Mississippi, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Utah, with Hawaii and Virginia taking effect in early 2025 and others adopting since. Check your state for the current status.
