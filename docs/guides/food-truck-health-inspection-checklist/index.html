Food truck health inspection checklist

Updated 2026-10-25 · 7 min read · Based on the FDA Food Code

Short answer: inspectors look at a food truck the same way they look at a restaurant, plus the mobile-specific items: a working hand sink with warm water, enough potable water, a wastewater tank larger than the water tank, and proof that you use an approved commissary or servicing area if your jurisdiction requires one. The rest is the usual: 41°F cold, 135°F hot, a calibrated thermometer, the right sanitizer strength, date marks and logs. Requirements vary, so check your local health department's mobile food checklist too.

Paperwork and people

  • Mobile food permit displayed, plus any commissary agreement or servicing log your jurisdiction requires.
  • A person in charge on the truck during every shift who can answer food safety questions (§2-101.11, §2-102.11).
  • A certified food protection manager, if your jurisdiction has adopted that requirement (§2-102.12).
  • Employee health: staff know to report symptoms such as vomiting, diarrhea or jaundice, and certain diagnoses (§2-201.11).
  • Your logs and any written procedures (for example TPHC) on board.

Hand washing and water

Hand sinkAccessible, not blocked or used for storage, warm water at least 85°F, soap, paper towels or other approved drying§5-202.12, §5-205.11, §6-301.12

Small truck fridges work hard on hot days with the door opening constantly. Check them before service and during the rush; see the food truck temperature log guide.

Thermometers

  • A probe thermometer accurate to ±2°F, including a thin probe if you cook thin foods like burgers (§4-203.11, §4-302.12).
  • A thermometer in each fridge, in the warmest part (§4-204.112).
  • Calibrated regularly. See how to calibrate a food thermometer.

Cleaning and sanitizing

  • A three-compartment sink or other approved warewashing set-up, if your jurisdiction requires it on the truck.
  • Sanitizer at the correct concentration, with test strips on board (§4-501.114, §4-302.14). Chlorine, for example, at 50–99 ppm in water at least 75°F (pH 8 or less) or 100°F (pH 10 or less). See sanitizer ppm.
  • Wiping cloths kept in sanitizer between uses (§3-304.14).
  • Food-contact surfaces used with TCS food cleaned at least every 4 hours (§4-602.11).

Food storage and labeling

  • Food from approved sources (§3-201.11), with receipts or invoices available.
  • Raw meat stored below ready-to-eat food.
  • Ready-to-eat TCS food held more than 24 hours date-marked for no more than 7 days at 41°F (§3-501.17). See the 7-day rule.
  • Food stored off the floor and covered, chemicals stored below and away from food.

Structure and pests

  • Clean, smooth, cleanable surfaces in good repair.
  • Serving window screened or otherwise protected from insects, if required locally.
  • No signs of pests; trash in covered containers.

Five-minute pre-service check

  1. Water tank full, wastewater tank empty, hand sink stocked and running warm.
  2. Fridges at 41°F or below; write it down.
  3. Hot holding at 135°F or above before food goes in.
  4. Sanitizer made and tested.
  5. Thermometer clean, calibrated and in your pocket.

Inspection results are often scored by priority, priority foundation and core items. Under the Food Code, priority items are generally corrected on the spot or within 72 hours, and priority foundation items within 10 calendar days (§8-405.11), though your jurisdiction may differ. FAQ

What do health inspectors check on a food truck?

Hand sink and water supply, wastewater tank, cold and hot holding temperatures, thermometers, sanitizer strength, food sources, date marking, cleanliness and your permit and commissary paperwork.

Does a food truck need a commissary?

Many jurisdictions require mobile units to operate from an approved commissary or servicing area for water, waste, cleaning and storage, but the rule is local. Ask your health department.

How big does a food truck wastewater tank need to be?

Under FDA Food Code §5-401.11, at least 15% larger than the water supply tank. Your jurisdiction may set specific sizes.
