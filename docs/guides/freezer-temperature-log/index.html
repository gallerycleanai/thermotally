Freezer temperature log: targets, thawing rules and a free printable sheet

Updated 2026-10-25 · 6 min read · Based on the FDA Food Code

Short answer: the FDA Food Code says frozen food must be kept frozen (§3-501.11). It doesn't print a freezer number, so most kitchens and health departments use 0°F (−18°C) or below as the working target. Log the freezer at least once or twice a day, note defrost cycles, and write what you did if food shows signs of thawing.

What the rules actually say

TopicFDA Food Code
Frozen storageStored frozen foods shall be maintained frozen (§3-501.11)
Receiving frozen foodReceived frozen (§3-202.11(E)); reject packages with signs of thawing and refreezing
Thermometer in the unitPlaced to measure the warmest part and easy to read (§4-204.112); air thermometers accurate to ±3°F (§4-203.12(B))
ThawingOnly by approved methods (§3-501.13): see below

The 0°F figure comes from food-safety agencies such as the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, which recommends keeping freezers at 0°F. It's a recommendation, not a Food Code limit. Some local health departments write their own number into their forms, so check yours.

How often to check a freezer

  • Reach-in and chest freezers: once or twice a day, usually opening and closing.
  • Walk-in freezers: twice a day, plus after large deliveries.
  • Any freezer after a power cut or repair: hourly until it's back at target.

A freezer that drifts from 0°F to 15°F is still "frozen" on paper, but it's a warning sign. Ice cream softens, product quality drops, and a failing compressor rarely recovers on its own. Logging the number, not just "OK", lets you spot the trend.

What to record

  1. Date and time
  2. Air temperature
  3. Initials
  4. Notes: defrost cycle, delivery, door left open, frost build-up
  5. Corrective action and re-check, if above your target

Defrost cycles: the most common "false alarm"

Commercial freezers run automatic defrost cycles several times a day. During a cycle the air reading can rise for a short time while the food stays solid. If you catch one, write the reading with "defrost" next to it and re-check after 30 minutes. If the box is still warm after the cycle ends, treat it as a real problem.

Signs of thaw and refreeze

When a freezer has been warm, look at the food, not just the thermometer:

  • Large ice crystals inside packages, or frozen liquid at the bottom of a case
  • Boxes that are stained, warped or stuck together
  • Items that have changed shape (meat that sagged and refroze, ice cream with an icy crust)

These are the same signs you check for when receiving frozen deliveries. Decide what to keep or discard with your manager and health department, and write it down.

Thawing: the part of freezer safety inspectors actually cite

Food Code §3-501.13 allows four ways to thaw TCS food:

MethodConditions
In the refrigeratorFood stays at 41°F or below
Under running waterFully submerged, water 70°F or below, enough flow to wash loose particles off; limits on how long thawed parts can sit above 41°F
As part of cookingCooked from frozen to its required temperature
In the microwaveOnly if it goes straight into cooking with no interruption

Thawing on the counter or in a sink of standing water isn't on the list. If you thaw a lot, add a "thawing" line to your prep log with the method and start time.

When the freezer fails

  1. Keep the door shut. A full freezer holds temperature far longer than an open one.
  2. Move product to another freezer or into a walk-in cooler if it's going to thaw anyway, and treat it as refrigerated food from that point.
  3. Probe the warmest items. Food that has thawed and gone above 41°F is now a cold-holding question, with the same rules as the fridge.
  4. Call for service and log hourly readings until it's back at target.

FAQ

What temperature should a commercial freezer be?

The FDA Food Code only requires frozen food to stay frozen. The common target is 0°F (−18°C) or below. Your local health department may set its own number, so check its forms or ask your inspector.

How often should freezer temperatures be logged?

No national frequency is set. Once or twice a day is common practice, more often after deliveries, power cuts or repairs.

Can I refreeze food that thawed in the refrigerator?

The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service says food thawed in the refrigerator can be refrozen without cooking, though quality may suffer. Food that went above 41°F is a different story; follow your health department's guidance.
