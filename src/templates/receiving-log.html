Receiving temperatures (FDA Food Code §3-202.11)

ProductReceive at
Refrigerated TCS food (meat, poultry, dairy, cut produce)41°F (5°C) or below
Milk, shell eggs, molluscan shellstockExceptions apply where other law sets the temperature (e.g. 45°F for milk and shellstock, then cooled to 41°F within 4 hours). See §3-202.11 and §3-501.14(C).
Hot TCS food135°F (57°C) or above
Frozen foodReceived frozen, with no signs of thawing and refreezing

How to use it

  1. Probe one or two items from each refrigerated delivery: between two packages, or inside the product for bulk items.
  2. Look for damaged, leaking or swollen packages, broken seals, ice crystals or frozen liquid (signs of thawing and refreezing), and missing labels or shellstock tags.
  3. Write the supplier, product, temperature, condition, use-by or lot, and accept or reject. Note the reason for any rejection.
  4. Put cold and frozen items away immediately.

FAQ

Do I have to log every delivery?

Requirements vary. Many HACCP plans and inspectors expect receiving checks for TCS food. A log also gives you evidence when you ask a supplier for credit on a rejected delivery.

How long should I keep shellstock tags?

The FDA Food Code requires shellstock identification tags to be kept for 90 days from the date the container is emptied (§3-203.12).
