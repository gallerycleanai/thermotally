What's on the schedule

  • Every shift / every 4 hours: food-contact surfaces, cutting boards and utensils in continuous use (FDA Food Code §4-602.11(C)), sanitizer buckets.
  • Daily: sinks, slicers and mixers after use, fridge handles and gaskets, grill and fryer exteriors, floors and drains, bins, restrooms, dish machine temperature or sanitizer check.
  • Weekly: walk-in floor and shelving, hood filters.
  • Monthly: ice machine interior (or per the maker's instructions), behind and under equipment.

Sanitizer checks

The Food Code requires a test kit to measure sanitizer concentration (§4-302.14). Follow the label on your sanitizer. Common ranges are about 50–100 ppm for chlorine and 200–400 ppm for quaternary ammonium, but the label is what counts. Write the reading in the Notes column.

FAQ

What should be on a restaurant cleaning schedule?

Each task, how often it's done, who does it, what chemical or method to use, and a place to sign. Group tasks by frequency (every 4 hours, daily, weekly, monthly) so nothing rare gets forgotten.

How often must food-contact surfaces be cleaned?

Under the FDA Food Code, surfaces and utensils in continuous use with TCS food must be cleaned at least every 4 hours, and also whenever you switch between raw animal foods and ready-to-eat foods.
