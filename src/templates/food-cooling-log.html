The cooling rule (FDA Food Code §3-501.14)

StageFrom → ToTime limit
Stage 1135°F → 70°F (57°C → 21°C)Within 2 hours
Stage 270°F → 41°F (21°C → 5°C)Within the next 4 hours (6 hours total)
Food made from room-temperature ingredients→ 41°FWithin 4 hours

The standard corrective action in FDA guidance: if stage 1 isn't met in 2 hours, the food can be reheated to 165°F and the cooling restarted, or discarded. If stage 2 isn't met by 6 hours, discard it.

How to use it

  1. When food comes off heat, write the date, item, cooling method (ice bath, shallow pans, ice wand, blast chiller), start time and temperature.
  2. Check at 1 hour. If it isn't well on its way to 70°F, speed it up: divide into shallow pans, stir, use an ice wand.
  3. At 2 hours, record the temperature. It must be 70°F or below.
  4. Record again at 4 hours and at 6 hours. It must be 41°F or below by 6 hours.

FAQ

Why are there two stages?

Bacteria grow fastest between about 125°F and 70°F, so the Food Code requires food to pass through that range quickly (2 hours), and then allows more time to get down to 41°F.

Do I have to log every batch?

Many health departments expect a cooling log for every batch of TCS food you cook and cool, such as rice, beans, soups, sauces and roasts. Some require it only after a violation. A log is the only proof you met the time limits.

Is putting a hot stockpot in the walk-in enough?

Usually not. Large, deep containers often can't reach 70°F in 2 hours, and they warm everything else in the walk-in. Use shallow pans, ice baths or ice wands.
