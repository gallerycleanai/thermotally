The rules this log follows

HoldingLimitIf it's out of range
Hot holding135°F (57°C) or above, §3-501.16(A)(1)Reheat to 165°F within 2 hours (§3-403.11) or discard. Many operators discard if they can't confirm how long it was below 135°F.
Cold holding41°F (5°C) or below, §3-501.16(A)(2)Rapidly chill and fix the unit, or discard, depending on how long it was above 41°F.

How to use it

  1. Check each held item with a calibrated probe thermometer. Probe the food itself, at the center, not the water bath.
  2. Check at least every 2 hours. The 2-hour interval is a common best practice because it leaves time to correct a problem.
  3. Write the date, time, item, hot or cold, temperature and Y/N for "in range".
  4. For any "N", write the corrective action (reheated to 168°F, moved to the walk-in, discarded) and initial it.

FAQ

What temperature should hot food be held at?

135°F (57°C) or above under the FDA Food Code. Some states still use 140°F, so check your local code.

How often should hot holding temperatures be checked?

The Food Code doesn't set a fixed interval. Every 2 hours is a widely recommended practice, and some HACCP plans check every hour.

Can I hold food without temperature control?

Only under "time as a public health control" (§3-501.19): food can be held up to 4 hours (or 6 hours for cold food under conditions) with written procedures and time marking, then must be cooked, served or discarded. Your health department may need to approve it.
