The two TPHC options (FDA Food Code §3-501.19)

4-hour option6-hour option (cold food only)
Starting temperature41°F or below, or 135°F or above (some cut fruit, vegetables and freshly opened sealed foods may start at 70°F or below)41°F or below
During holdingNo temperature limitFood must never go above 70°F, and you must monitor it (or keep the room cool enough that it can't)
MarkingThe time 4 hours after removalThe removal time and the time 6 hours later
At the endCook and serve, serve, or discard within 4 hoursCook and serve, serve, or discard within 6 hours; discard immediately if above 70°F
Unmarked or past timeDiscard

Either way, you need written procedures kept on site and available to the inspector (§3-501.19(A)). Many health departments also want to see or approve them, and some don't allow TPHC for certain foods. Facilities serving a highly susceptible population (such as nursing homes) have extra limits, including on raw eggs (§3-501.19(D)).

How to use the log

  1. Write one line per pan or batch when it leaves the hot well, cold rail or walk-in.
  2. Probe it and write the starting temperature. If it doesn't meet the starting temperature, it can't go on TPHC.
  3. Write the discard-by time on the log and on the pan (tape, sticker or color tag).
  4. At the discard time, record what happened: served, cooked, or discarded, and the time.

FAQ

Can I put food back in the fridge after using TPHC?

No. The Food Code lists only three outcomes within the time limit: cook and serve, serve, or discard. Putting the food back in the fridge to "reset the clock" isn't one of them.

Do I need my health department's permission?

The Food Code requires written procedures. Whether you also need approval depends on your state or local code, so ask your inspector before you start.
