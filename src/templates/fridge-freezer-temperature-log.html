What's on the sheet

  • Header: business, unit name/location, month, thermometer ID
  • 31 rows, one per day: AM time, AM temperature, initials, PM time, PM temperature, initials
  • Corrective action, re-check temperature and manager sign-off columns
  • The limits printed at the top so whoever's on shift doesn't have to remember them

Temperatures to aim for

UnitLimitSource
Refrigerator / walk-in / prep cooler41°F (5°C) or belowFDA Food Code §3-501.16(A)(2)
FreezerFood must stay frozen; 0°F (−18°C) or below is the usual target§3-501.11 (0°F is an industry best practice, not a Code number)

Many operators set fridges to 36–38°F so normal door openings don't push food above 41°F.

How to use it

  1. Print one sheet per unit per month and tape it to the door or keep it on a clipboard nearby.
  2. Read the thermometer at opening and closing (or as your health department requires). Write the time, temperature and your initials.
  3. If it's above 41°F: check the food temperature with a probe, move TCS food to another unit if needed, fix the cause (door left open, overloaded, defrost cycle), and re-check within 30 minutes. Write what you did.
  4. Food that has been above 41°F for too long may need to be discarded. Ask your health department for its rule; many use 4 hours.
  5. File completed sheets and keep them for as long as your health department requires.

FAQ

How often should fridge temperatures be checked?

The FDA Food Code sets the 41°F limit, not a schedule. At least twice a day (opening and closing) is the most common expectation, and many HACCP plans check every 4 hours. Your local health department may set its own frequency.

Should I record the air temperature or the food temperature?

Most logs record the unit's air temperature from a thermometer placed in the warmest part of the unit. If the reading is high, probe the food itself, because the food temperature is what the 41°F rule is about.

Can I use this sheet for a pharmacy or vaccine fridge?

No. Vaccine and medication storage has different ranges (typically 36–46°F for refrigerated vaccines) and different rules. This sheet is for food.
