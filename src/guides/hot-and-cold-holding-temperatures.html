The danger zone is between 41°F and 135°F. Holding food out of it during service is the daily job of every steam table, hot well, salad bar and prep rail, and it should be in your logs.

The two numbers

FDA Food CodeSection
Hot holding135°F (57°C) or above§3-501.16(A)(1)
Cold holding41°F (5°C) or below§3-501.16(A)(2)

A few jurisdictions still use different numbers (for example 140°F hot holding in some older codes). Always check the code your health department has adopted.

Getting food into hot holding the right way

  • Cook to the required temperature first (for example poultry 165°F, ground beef 155°F, §3-401.11).
  • Food that was cooked, cooled and is being reheated for hot holding must reach 165°F within 2 hours (§3-403.11). Steam tables and hot wells are for holding, not reheating.
  • Preheat the hot well before loading it.

How often to check

The Code doesn't fix a frequency. Every 2 hours is a common recommendation because it leaves time to fix a problem: if hot food is found below 135°F and was last checked at 135°F or above under 2 hours ago, it can usually be reheated to 165°F. Many HACCP plans use 1–2 hours for hot holding and 2–4 hours for cold rails.

Always probe the food, in the center and in more than one pan, not the water in the well.

Corrective actions

SituationCommon action
Hot food below 135°F, last good check less than 2 h agoReheat to 165°F, then return to holding
Hot food below 135°F, unknown time or more than 2 hDiscard
Cold food above 41°F, last good check less than 4 h agoChill rapidly to 41°F; fix the unit
Cold food above 41°F, unknown time or more than 4 hDiscard

These are common practices taught in food safety training. Your health department's rules decide.

Time instead of temperature (TPHC)

Under "time as a public health control" (§3-501.19) you can hold food without temperature control for up to 4 hours, then cook, serve or discard it. There's also a 6-hour option for cold food that starts at 41°F or below and never goes above 70°F. You need written procedures, and each pan must be marked with its discard time. Some jurisdictions require approval first. Food trucks and buffets use TPHC a lot, and it needs its own log.

FAQ

What's the danger zone?

Between 41°F and 135°F (5°C–57°C), where bacteria grow fastest. Holding, cooling and reheating rules are all designed to keep TCS food out of that range or move it through quickly.

Does the salad bar need to be logged?

Yes, if it holds TCS food such as cut greens, cut tomatoes, cut melon, dairy or cooked items. Treat it like any cold holding unit.
