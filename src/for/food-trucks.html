Why trucks need something different

No signal at the fairground

Logging has to work offline and sync later. Most restaurant apps assume Wi-Fi.

More than one jurisdiction

Trucks that cross city or county lines may answer to several health departments, each with its own permit and checklist. One record, many inspectors.

Tiny team, no office

It's often just two people. Logging has to take seconds, not a laptop session after midnight.

Food truck FAQ

Does a food truck need temperature logs?

Most health departments expect mobile vendors to hold food at the same temperatures as restaurants (41°F or below cold, 135°F or above hot) and to show they're monitoring it. Whether a written log is mandatory depends on your jurisdiction and your menu, so ask your local health department. A log is your best evidence either way.

Does a food truck need a commissary?

Many US jurisdictions require a mobile food unit to operate from an approved commissary or base of operation for water, wastewater, storage and cleaning. Rules vary by state and county, so check with the health department that issues your permit.

What's on a food truck health inspection checklist?

Typical items include holding temperatures, a working probe thermometer, handwashing sink with hot water, soap and towels, water and wastewater tank capacity, sanitizer at the right concentration, food from approved sources, and your permit posted.
