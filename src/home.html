How it works

Three steps. No sensors, no training day.

Most small kitchens already own a probe thermometer and a clipboard. {{BRAND}} replaces the clipboard, not your thermometer.

1

Log on your phone in 10 seconds

Tap the unit, type the temperature, done. Your limits are built in (41°F cold, 135°F hot, the 2-hour/6-hour cooling rule), so a bad reading is flagged right away and asks for a corrective action.

2

Get a nudge when a check is missed

Set when each check is due: opening, closing, every 2 or 4 hours. If nobody logs it, the person on shift gets a reminder, and so do you. No more filling in the whole week at once before an inspection.

3

Print an inspector-ready PDF

Pick a date range and get one tidy report with every reading, who took it, the time, and what was done when something was out of range. Email it, print it, or show it on the phone.

Prefer to keep paper? We're also testing a "snap your sheet" option: fill in our printable log as usual, take a photo at closing, and {{BRAND}} files it by date and flags blanks and out-of-range numbers. Tell us on the waitlist if you'd use it.

The numbers that matter

Built on the FDA Food Code

Most US states base their food code on the FDA Food Code. These are its core temperature rules, which {{BRAND}} will use as defaults. You can change them to match your local code.

CheckFDA Food Code limitSection
Cold holding (fridges, prep rails)41°F (5°C) or below§3-501.16(A)(2)

FAQ

Is {{BRAND}} available now?

Not yet. {{BRAND}} is in early development. Join the waitlist to get early access and a founding-member price. The printable templates are free and available today.

Do I need special thermometers or sensors?

No. You keep using the calibrated probe or appliance thermometer you already have and type the reading on your phone. Wireless sensors may come later as an option, but they will never be required.

Will a digital log be accepted by my health inspector?

Most health departments care that records are complete, accurate, dated and available on request, not whether they are on paper. Some jurisdictions require a specific form or format, so check with your local health department. {{BRAND}} will export a PDF you can print or email.

How often should I check fridge temperatures?

The FDA Food Code sets the limit (41°F or below) but not a fixed frequency. Many health departments and HACCP plans expect at least twice a day, at opening and closing, and more often for prep coolers that are opened all day.

Does it work for food trucks without good signal?

That's the plan: logging will work offline and sync when you're back online, and one truck can keep logs for its commissary and several counties.

What will it cost?

We're aiming for a simple flat price per location, per month, with no setup fee and no annual contract. The waitlist asks what you'd pay, and that answer decides the final price.
